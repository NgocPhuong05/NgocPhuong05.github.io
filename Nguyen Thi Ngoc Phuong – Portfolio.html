Big Data Analytics and Artificial Intelligence in Monitoring Public Investment Expenditure: Towards Smart Public Financial Governance

With Dr. Le Thi Ngoc (State Treasury of Vietnam, Ministry of Finance)

Abstract

Public investment expenditure (PIE) from the state budget in Vietnam during 2021–2025 has stayed at roughly 26–32% of total state budget expenditure, or over 6–7% of GDP a year, confirming the central role of public investment in growth and infrastructure. In practice, however, slow disbursement, adjustments to total investment capital, fragmented allocation, and risks of inefficiency and leakage persist.

In the context of national digital transformation and the need to strengthen fiscal discipline, this study analyzes the potential of Big Data analytics (BDA) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) for monitoring PIE, aiming at a Smart Public Financial Management (Smart PFM) model. It uses institutional analysis, secondary data synthesis and international comparison to develop an integrated framework linking state budget data, public investment management and e-procurement systems.

The results point to three groups of high-potential AI tools: (i) anomaly detection in expenditure transactions; (ii) machine-learning-based project risk scoring; and (iii) disbursement forecasting for proactive fiscal management. Effective implementation requires robust data governance, algorithm transparency and clear accountability. The study proposes a Smart Treasury Platform and a comprehensive public financial data governance framework suited to the digital economy.

Keywords: Big Data analytics, AI, public investment expenditure, public financial management, fiscal oversight, intelligent governance, digital government.

AI and Financial Innovation in Risk Management and Investment: Theory, Practice, and Lessons for Vietnam

With Dr. Le Thi Ngoc · International Conference Proceedings: AI and Innovations in Financial Market Analysis, NEU Publishing House, 2025

Abstract

In the context of digital transformation, artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping financial decision-making, enhancing forecasting, optimising portfolio management and improving corporate governance. Machine learning, big data analytics and blockchain have marked a turning point in financial risk assessment and global investment strategies. In Vietnam, however, adoption remains limited because of weak data infrastructure, a shortage of AI-skilled professionals and an underdeveloped regulatory framework, even though the potential in banking, insurance and capital markets is strong.

This study examines AI as a driver of financial innovation in risk management and investment, covering theoretical foundations, international best practices and policy implications for Vietnam. It recommends a national financial data infrastructure, a “legal sandbox” for AI-FinTech innovation, and investment in digital financial human capital. Collaboration among regulators, financial institutions and technology enterprises is identified as a key condition for sustainable AI adoption.

The research is qualitative, with comparative analysis and synthesis of experience from the United States, the European Union, Japan, China and Singapore, drawing on academic studies, institutional reports and legal guidelines. It offers an integrated view connecting AI-driven innovation with financial risk management and investment in transitional economies, and practical insights for Vietnam's transition toward a transparent, adaptive and technology-driven financial system.

Keywords: AI, financial innovation, risk management, investment, FinTech.

International Experience and Lessons for the Vietnam State Treasury on a Two-Tier Organizational Model

With Dr. Le Thi Ngoc · National Scientific Conference “Two-Tier Local Government Model: The Role of Commune-Level Government, the New Grassroots Unit”, NEU, 2025 (original title in Vietnamese)

Abstract (English translation)

This paper presents how State Treasury models are organized and operated in several countries and draws lessons for Vietnam in implementing a two-tier State Treasury model. The model is consistent with the strategy for developing the finance sector and the State Treasury system, and with the orientation toward a modern, safe and efficient digital Treasury in line with international practice and the Fourth Industrial Revolution. It follows Conclusion No. 09-KL/BCĐ of 24 November 2024 of the Central Steering Committee, which aims to build and operate a two-tier treasury model matching the organization of local government and administrative areas in the new period.

Keywords: State Treasury, two-tier local government.

Assessing Vietnam's 2024 Economic Developments through the Finance and Budget Sector and Proposing Solutions for 2025

With Dr. Le Thi Ngoc and Dr. Bui Thi Hong Viet · National Scientific Conference “Vietnam's Economy in 2024 and Outlook for 2025: Promoting Economic Institutional Reform in the New Context” (original title in Vietnamese)

Abstract

In 2024 Vietnam's economy, despite many challenges, achieved impressive growth: GDP rose 7.09% to VND 11,511.9 trillion (USD 476.3 billion), and GDP per capita reached VND 114 million (about USD 4,700), up USD 377 from the previous year. The finance and budget sector recorded many positive results that lay a foundation for future development. Stock market capitalization rose 20.6% from the end of 2023, and total state budget revenue reached VND 2,037.5 trillion, 19.8% above the estimate and up 16.2% year on year, while budget expenditure was reasonably controlled at VND 1,830.8 trillion, up 5.7%.

The State Bank of Vietnam remains committed to a flexible monetary policy to control inflation, while monitoring global economic policies to adjust domestic strategy when needed. Difficulties remain, however, including slow growth in domestic consumption: retail sales and consumer services rose only 9.0%, lower than in 2023, which weighs on aggregate demand.

Keywords: economy, finance, budget.

Solutions to State Financial Reports Completion According to International Practices

With Dr. Le Thi Ngoc · 7th International Conference on Finance, Accounting and Auditing (ICFAA 2024), Hanoi, 21 December 2024

Abstract

Accounting Law No. 88/2015/QH13, effective from 1 January 2017, introduced new regulations on the annual preparation of State Financial Statements (SFS). Together with Decree No. 25/2018/ND-CP on SFS and related documents, it led the Ministry of Finance to begin officially preparing SFS from 2019. However, SFS still has limitations and does not fully meet international practice on scope, principles, reasonableness, reliability, consistency, timeliness, completeness and transparency.

To strengthen the role and effectiveness of SFS in managing and operating the state budget, and to provide state financial information that is complete, honest, timely and reliable, this article assesses the current status of the first SFS (2018–2022) and proposes comprehensive solutions and recommendations to competent state agencies to bring SFS in line with international practice.

Keywords: accounting, finance, state financial reports, state financial statements (SFS). JEL code: M41.
